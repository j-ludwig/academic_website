Multivariate Analysis

Change in Notation

Some symbols in Chapters 1 to 5 have changed, so that every symbol now has one meaning throughout the notes. The ideas, the examples and the results are the same. This page lists the changes chapter by chapter. The figures and sliders use the new symbols as well.

Conventions

  • Indices. Observations are numbered \(s\) and \(t\), variables \(i\) and \(j\), and factors, components and dimensions \(l\). The number of components or dimensions that are kept is \(k\).
  • Bold. Bold lower-case letters are observations, such as \(\mathbf x_t\), \(\boldsymbol\varepsilon_t\) and \(\mathbf f_t\). Bold capitals are matrices with one row for each observation: the data matrix \(\mathbf X\), and in Chapter 5 also \(\mathbf D\), \(\mathbf B\), \(\mathbf J\), \(\mathbf V\) and \(\mathbf F\).
  • Rotations. A rotation matrix is \(Q=Q(\theta)\), turning by the angle \(\theta\) (theta). A turned matrix or shock gets a tilde, as in \(\tilde A=AQ\) and \(\tilde{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}=Q'\boldsymbol\varepsilon\).
  • Eigenvalues. An eigenvalue is \(\kappa\) (kappa), as in \(Sv=\kappa v\). The letter \(\lambda\) is kept for the loadings of a factor model.
  • Splitting \(A\). \(A=C\Delta\): the diagonal matrix \(\Delta\) (capital delta) holds the standard deviations \(\delta_l\) of the factors, and \(C\) how the variables respond to one unit of each factor. In PCA, \(C\) is written \(\Gamma\).
